# Research playbook

Load this when the task is research: comparisons, roundups, "what changed", fact-finding.

## Split
- One slice per topic, product or source, each a Haiku agent.
- Add a **business, pricing and deals** slice by default (pricing, packaging, acquisitions, partnerships). It is the slice most often missed.
- Add a **breadth (roundup) scan** in the same first wave: a Haiku agent that lists every major item from 2–3 recap or overview sources. It can also cover basics such as dates.
- Stay within 5 agents at once by merging small slices.

## Brief traps to name
- Official pages that don't render through fetch. Give fallbacks: official page → help or docs site → official blog → reputable press, labelled as press.
- Web searches that return only links, so the agent must fetch the best one or two.
- Ask for exact figures and short excerpts with source URLs, not summaries.

## After the first wave
1. Compare the roundup scan against the slices once. Real gaps get one short follow-up; nothing else does.
2. Flagged or conflicting items: one or two the boss checks inline; more than that goes to a verifier.

## Fast-changing facts (prices, stock, deals)
- Haiku gathers them, with the date seen and the source.
- **Every price is verified by Opus or Sonnet** against a live page before anyone relies on it. Cheap agents tend to pass old prices off as current and give up on pages that do load.
- Shopping questions with only a few candidates are usually cheaper and more accurate done directly (principle 1).

## Verifier (Sonnet), decided per task
- Use one when the research will be relied on or shared and the cheap agents covered a broad area, hit failed or secondary sources, or disagreed.
- Tell the user what it would catch and roughly what it adds in cost and time.
- Scope it to flagged, conflicting or high-impact items. Size its budget to the scope (see principle 4) and top it up immediately if it comes back short.

## Why this exists
Boss review catches errors in what it's shown, but it can't see what nobody looked for. In measured runs, skipping the breadth check missed the biggest news, while a cheap verifier caught wrong dates and missing items.
